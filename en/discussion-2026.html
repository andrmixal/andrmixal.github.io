transcription and translation

Author: Vadym Kondratiuk, Doctor of Medical Sciences, Senior Researcher, Shalimov National Scientific Center of Surgery and Transplantology (per the center's website). source

A strange article, more likely a translation from English.

May-Thurner syndrome is a well-known disease. Ukraine learned to diagnose and treat it endovascularly quite a long time ago; the first stenting for it in our clinic took place somewhere in the mid-90s (IVUS was not even in the pipeline then). Stenting of a compressed left iliac vein is now available, and has been for about 20 years. It is not a very complex intervention, and there was never an absolute need for IVUS in it.

Now IVUS is available in many clinics with an X-ray operating room; it is no longer anything exclusive. The specialists exist. Venous stents exist.

The only problem is purely logistical: the NHSU does not purchase these stents (they are quite expensive, and there are no cheap Chinese analogues), and does not allow patients to buy them themselves (co-payment). So such operations are performed mostly in private centers.

3. Facebook comment, screenshot (scan)

transcription and translation

Author: Kostiantyn Nadutyi, Deputy Chair of the Board, All-Ukrainian Medical Society, VULT (per the society's website). source

Unfortunately, planning is not based on an analysis of disease burden and the need for interventions, but on the volumes of services provided under the PMG (Programme of Medical Guarantees) in the previous year. That is, if such a service was not provided before, it should not appear in the plan for next year. To push this through, I do not even know what effort is needed, because the source of management information (the old statistical records) has been abolished and no new system has been created. This is advantageous from the point of view of saving the budget, and so even preventive stenting of the coronary arteries did not get into the PMG right away, despite being obvious. The key point here is "not profitable", and the benefit is considered purely financial and [purely] for the budget.
